abstract: Decision theoretical troubleshooting is about minimizing the expected cost
  of solving a certain problem like repairing a complicated man-made device. In this
  paper we consider situations where you have to take apart some of the device to
  get access to certain clusters and actions. Specifically, we investigate troubleshooting
  with independent actions in a tree of clusters where actions inside a cluster
  cannot be performed before the cluster is opened. The problem is non-trivial because
  there is a cost associated with opening and closing a cluster. Troubleshooting with
  independent actions and no clusters can be solved in $O(n \cdot lg n)$ time ($n$ being
  the number of actions) by the well-known ”P-over-C” algorithm due to Kadane and
  Simon, but an efficient and optimal algorithm for a tree cluster model has not
  yet been found. In this paper we describe a ”bottom-up P-over-C” O(n \cdot lg n)
  time algorithm and show that it is optimal when the clusters do not need to be closed
  to test whether the actions solved the problem.
