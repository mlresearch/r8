abstract: We consider two variables that are related to each other by an invertible
  function. While it has previously been shown that the dependence structure of
  the noise can provide hints to determine which of the two variables is the cause,
  we presently show that even in the deterministic (noise-free) case, there are
  asymmetries that can be exploited for causal inference. Our method is based
  on the idea that if the function and the probability density of the cause are
  chosen independently, then the distribution of the effect will, in a certain sense,
  depend on the function. We provide a theoretical analysis of this method, showing
  that it also works in the low noise regime, and link it to information geometry.
  We report strong empirical results on various real-world data sets from different
  domains.
